The philosophy behind the work

A lesson from the Tao Te Ching

The title draws from Chapter 78 of the Tao Te Ching, which reflects on water's ability to overcome what is hard and strong. Water yields rather than contests hardness on its own terms, yet its persistence can transform what seems immovable.

In this generative experiment, flowing ink represents the Soft: adaptive, continuous, and responsive. Geometric structures represent the Hard: fixed forms that resist the flow but gradually erode through repeated contact. The ink's movement and accumulating traces evoke a natural process rather than a single decisive act. The simulation offers a visual meditation on yielding, persistence, resistance, and change—not a proof of a philosophical principle. Consider: What forms of strength become possible when we stop equating strength with resistance?
